COMPUTATIONAL METHODOLOGY
==========================

2.X Quantum Chemical and QM/MM Analysis of Inhibition Mechanism

2.X.1 Protein–Ligand Complex Preparation

The protein–ligand complex structures were obtained from molecular docking 
and molecular dynamics simulation studies. The binding poses were extracted 
from the lowest energy minima conformations. Four inhibitor complexes with 
HisA enzyme were subjected to quantum chemical analysis: HisA_F0001-2392, 
HisA_F3368-0104, HisA_F9995-0189, and HisA_Reference. The binding pocket 
was defined as all protein residues within 8.0 Å of the ligand, yielding 
68 pocket residues (587 MM atoms) with AMBER ff14SB partial charges assigned 
to each protein atom.

2.X.2 Density Functional Theory (DFT) Calculations

All quantum chemical calculations were performed using PySCF (version 2.14.0) 
[CITATION] with GPU acceleration via GPU4PySCF (version 1.8.1) [CITATION] 
on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 Ti GPU. The ligand geometry was extracted 
directly from the protein–ligand complex PDB coordinates.

Ground-state electronic structure calculations were carried out using 
Kohn–Sham DFT with the B3LYP hybrid exchange-correlation functional 
[CITATION: Becke 1993, Lee et al. 1988] and the def2-SVP basis set 
[CITATION: Weigend & Ahlrichs 2005]. The SCF convergence threshold was 
set to 1×10⁻⁹ Hartree. All calculations employed the unrestricted 
Kohn–Sham (UKS) formalism.

The frontier molecular orbital energies (HOMO and LUMO) were obtained 
directly from the converged Kohn–Sham orbital eigenvalues. The vertical 
ionization potential (IP) and electron affinity (EA) were approximated 
using Koopmans' theorem as:

    IP = −E(HOMO)
    EA = −E(LUMO)

The following global chemical reactivity descriptors were derived from 
the frontier orbital energies [CITATION: Parr & Yang 1989]:

    Electronegativity:      χ = (IP + EA) / 2
    Chemical Hardness:      η = (IP − EA) / 2
    Global Softness:        S = 1 / (2η)
    Electrophilicity Index: ω = χ² / (2η)
    Maximum Charge Transfer: ΔN_max = χ / (2η)

2.X.3 Fukui Function Analysis

Condensed Fukui indices were calculated using the finite-difference 
approximation based on three separate DFT calculations on the neutral (N), 
anionic (N+1), and cationic (N−1) states of the ligand [CITATION: Yang & 
Mortier 1986]:

    f⁺(r) = ρ_N+1(r) − ρ_N(r)    [electrophilic attack sites]
    f⁻(r) = ρ_N(r) − ρ_N−1(r)    [nucleophilic attack sites]
    f⁰(r) = [f⁺(r) + f⁻(r)] / 2  [radical attack sites]
    Δf(r) = f⁺(r) − f⁻(r)        [dual descriptor]

Mulliken population analysis was employed to condense the Fukui functions 
to individual atoms. The atomic populations were computed using the 
standard Mulliken scheme:

    q_A = Z_A − Σ_(μ∈A) [PS]_μμ

where P is the density matrix, S is the overlap matrix, and Z_A is 
the nuclear charge of atom A.

2.X.4 Molecular Electrostatic Potential (MEP)

The molecular electrostatic potential was computed on a three-dimensional 
grid (resolution: 0.4 Å) surrounding the ligand van der Waals surface. 
The MEP was evaluated as:

    V(r) = Σ_A Z_A/|r − R_A| − ∫ ρ(r')/|r − r'| dr'

where the nuclear contribution was computed analytically and the electronic 
contribution was evaluated numerically using the DFT electron density 
ρ(r) obtained from the converged B3LYP/def2-SVP wavefunction. Grid points 
inside the van der Waals surface were excluded. Nucleophilic sites 
(V_min) and electrophilic sites (V_max) were identified from the MEP 
surface extrema.

2.X.5 QM/MM Electrostatic Embedding

The protein–ligand interaction was treated using a hybrid QM/MM approach 
with electrostatic embedding [CITATION: Warshel & Levitt 1976]. The ligand 
was treated as the QM region at the B3LYP/def2-SVP level, while the 
surrounding binding pocket residues (within 8.0 Å) were represented as 
MM point charges using AMBER ff14SB partial charges.

The QM Hamiltonian was modified to include the electrostatic field of 
the MM environment:

    H_QM/MM = H_QM + Σ_M Q_M / |r − R_M|

where Q_M are the AMBER partial charges of MM atoms at positions R_M. 
This electrostatic embedding was implemented using the PySCF qmmm module 
(pyscf.qmmm.mm_charge). The total QM/MM interaction energy was computed as:

    E_total = E_QM(embedded) + E_elec(classical) + E_LJ(vdW)

where E_QM(embedded) is the SCF energy of the QM region in the presence 
of MM point charges, E_elec(classical) is the classical Coulomb interaction 
(332.06 kcal/mol·e²/Å scaling factor), and E_LJ is the Lennard-Jones 
van der Waals contribution (ε = 0.12 kcal/mol, σ = 3.4 Å).

Per-residue energy decomposition was performed by computing the 
electrostatic and van der Waals contributions of each binding pocket 
residue to the total interaction energy.

2.X.6 Hydrogen Bond Analysis

Protein–ligand hydrogen bonds were identified using a geometric criterion: 
donor–acceptor distance ≤ 3.5 Å. Hydrogen bond donors and acceptors 
were identified from nitrogen and oxygen atoms, with donor/acceptor 
character assigned based on Mulliken partial charges from the DFT 
calculation. Hydrogen bonds with donor–acceptor distances < 3.0 Å 
were classified as strong, 3.0–3.2 Å as moderate, and 3.2–3.5 Å as weak.

2.X.7 Drug-likeness Assessment

Drug-likeness was evaluated using Lipinski's Rule of Five [CITATION: 
Lipinski et al. 2001]: molecular weight ≤ 500 Da, LogP ≤ 5, hydrogen 
bond donors ≤ 5, and hydrogen bond acceptors ≤ 10. The Quantitative 
Estimate of Drug-likeness (QED) score [CITATION: Bickerton et al. 2012] 
and topological polar surface area (TPSA) were computed using RDKit 
(version 2026.3.6) [CITATION].

2.X.8 Software and Hardware

All calculations were performed using:
- PySCF 2.14.0 for quantum chemical calculations
- GPU4PySCF 1.8.1 for GPU-accelerated DFT
- RDKit 2026.3.6 for cheminformatics
- Biopython 1.84 for PDB parsing
- NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib for data analysis and visualization
- Hardware: Intel CPU (24 threads), 128 GB RAM, 
  2× NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3070 Ti (8 GB VRAM each),
  Ubuntu Linux
